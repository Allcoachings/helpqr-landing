HelpQR's Inactivity Monitor Explained

HelpQR's inactivity monitor is a passive dead-man switch built specifically for India's solo-living population. Here is how it works:

  • You set the window: Choose any interval from 1 hour to 24 hours based on your routine. Night-shift workers typically choose 8–12 hours. Elderly users often use 2–4 hours.
  • The monitor watches quietly: HelpQR uses the phone's low-power sensor hub to track any phone interaction — a screen touch, a call, a movement. This uses less than 1% battery per day.
  • If the window expires: HelpQR sends an alert to your Help Circle with your last GPS fix, a timestamp and a message: "No activity detected — please check on [Name]."
  • SMS fallback guaranteed: Whether or not there is 4G, 3G or even 2G data, the alert goes out via SMS with a Google Maps link.

This is fundamentally different from apps like Life360 or Google Family Sharing that only work when you actively share location. HelpQR protects people who cannot act.

Daily Check-In — A Simple Tap That Means Everything

Beyond the inactivity monitor, HelpQR's daily check-in is a simple "I'm OK" button. Set a daily deadline — say, 8 AM every morning. If you tap the button before 8 AM, nothing happens. If you don't, HelpQR sends your family an alert at 8:05 AM with your location. Simple, zero-friction, life-saving.

Key Advantage

HelpQR's inactivity monitor triggers even if the person is unconscious and cannot touch their phone — the primary failure mode of all SOS button-based apps. It is the only free app in India with this passive protection layer.
